# Loudness fill: notes

## Not filled

- **afrobeat** (Fela / Africa '70 lineage), both features. No loudness measurement of afrobeat releases, original or reissued, was found. freshlybakedstudios' "Afrobeats" bucket covers the modern Nigerian pop genre, which is a different genre, and the survey doesn't say how its genre engine files Fela-style records. The genre has no measured parent to infer from (highlife, jazz and funk are all unmeasured or only inferred here), so any range would be a guess. Crest is left out too, because the fit below needs a loudness range to start from.
- **blues**, both features. No survey or database measures blues. lufs.to has no blues artists (Muddy Waters, B.B. King and Stevie Ray Vaughan all return 404), and freshlybakedstudios has no blues bucket. Modern blues ships through rock, singer/songwriter and jazz channels, whose medians run from -9.2 to -12.8 LUFS. Nothing picks one of those as the parent, so a range would be a guess.

## Caveats on what was filled

- **Crest scale vs the existing pop/rock/trap entries.** The app's `MixMeter.crestDB` is sample peak minus unweighted whole-track RMS, with no +3 dB AES offset. lufs.to's "dynamic range" is the same quantity (https://lufs.to/methodology). On that scale, lufs.to measures its trap at a median of 10.8 dB (at -6.9 LUFS), its pop at 12.5-14.6 dB and its tech house at 8.9-10.1 dB. On 2,544 tracks, crest runs about 2.1 dB (IQR 1.5-2.7) above PLR. The existing ranges are pop 5-10, rock 6-11 and trap 6-9, taken from iZotope's "5 dB or less" remark. On the app's own metric those look 3-5 dB too low. Worth rechecking, since they would make the Engineer call ordinary loud masters "squashed".
- **Most crest ranges are inferred from a pooled fit**: crest = 5.28 - 0.743 x LUFS (r = -0.88, residual SD 1.36 dB), from lufs.to per-track data, mapped over each genre's LUFS range. Only house (2010s subset), breakbeat and synth-pop use genre-specific lufs.to measurements. The fit ignores genre effects. For example, modern house measures about 1.3 dB below the fit at the same loudness, so the fit may overstate crest for four-on-the-floor club genres (amapiano, UK garage, jersey club, disco, techno).
- lufs.to's peak may be the true peak. If so, its crest reads up to about 0.5 dB above the app's sample-peak crest.
- **Parent-genre LUFS inferences** (amapiano, UK garage, jersey club, disco, funk, samba, bossa nova) all use freshlybakedstudios' interquartile ranges for released masters from 2012-2026. They describe what a producer releases today, not original-era masters. No original-era (1950s-70s) measurement exists for disco, funk, samba or bossa nova. lufs.to's "Funk" page is funk rock/metal (RHCP, Faith No More, 311), and its "Disco" page holds one 2001 Moroder album, so neither was used for these genres.
- **lo-fi hip-hop LUFS** (-14 to -12) is cited from LuvLang's genre guide. That is a practitioner's statement of where the genre sits, not a measured survey. Two other pages carrying lo-fi figures (lordreverb.com and veniamastering.studio) returned 403 and are not cited.
- **Amapiano** combines a measured parent (House IQR) with a tutorial's genre-specific target (BeatKey, -11 to -9). No amapiano-specific measurement was found.
- The lufs.to per-track numbers were read from its genre pages and the release pages they link (HTML pages only; nothing was downloaded as files).
